# Beef Bulgogi

Prep Time: 15 minutes

Cook Time: 15 minutes

Servings: 4–6

## Ingredients

- [ ] 1.5 lbs Ground Beef (80/20 or 90/10 lean)
- [ ] 2 large Carrots, shredded or julienned
- [ ] 4 cups Green Cabbage, thinly shredded (about half a small head)
- [ ] 1/4 cup Soy Sauce
- [ ] 2 tbsp Brown Sugar
- [ ] 2 tbsp Toasted Sesame Oil
- [ ] 3 cloves Garlic, minced
- [ ] 1 tsp Fresh Ginger, grated
- [ ] 1/4 cup Grated Asian Pear (Optional, but adds great moisture)
- [ ] 2 Green Onions, thinly sliced
- [ ] 1 tbsp Toasted Sesame Seeds
- [ ] 1/2 tsp Black Pepper
- [ ] 1 tbsp Vegetable Oil

## Instructions

- Whisk the Sauce: In a small bowl, combine the soy sauce, brown sugar, sesame oil, garlic, ginger, pear, and black pepper. Set aside.
- Brown the Beef: Heat the vegetable oil in a large skillet or wok over high heat. Add the ground beef and spread it out. Let it sit undisturbed for 2 minutes to get a deep, dark sear.
- Add the Aromatics: Break up the meat with a wooden spoon. Add the carrots to the pan and stir-fry for 2 minutes.
- Wilt the Cabbage: Add the shredded cabbage to the pan. It will look like a lot, but don't worry—it shrinks significantly as it cooks!
- Sauté: Toss the cabbage and carrots with the beef for 3–4 minutes. To sauté means to cook the food quickly over high heat; you want the cabbage to be tender but still have a slight "snap" or bite to it.
- Glaze: Pour the sauce mixture over the entire pan. Stir constantly for 2–3 minutes until the sauce reduces into a thick, sticky glaze that coats every strand of cabbage and carrot.
- Garnish: Toss in the green onions and sesame seeds just before serving.

## Notes

- Chef's Tips: The "Chiffonade" Technique
- To get those perfect, restaurant-style cabbage shreds, use the Chiffonade technique. Cut your cabbage into quarters and remove the core. Stack a few leaves on top of each other, roll them up tightly like a cigar, and then slice across the roll into very thin ribbons. This ensures the cabbage cooks evenly and mixes perfectly with the beef and carrots!
- Substitutions
- If you can't find an asian pear, a sweet, crisp apple like a Fuji, Gala, or Honeycrisp is the closest match for flavor and texture.
- Shortcut: If you're in a hurry, you can use a pre-packaged bag of "Coleslaw Mix" (cabbage and carrots) from the produce aisle.
- Spicy Kick: Add a teaspoon of red chili flakes or a squeeze of Sriracha to the sauce if you like a little heat with your crunch.
